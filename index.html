                What to do

                About Ready Map

                Ready Map is part of Ready, an emergency-prep helper. It works anywhere in the world. It shows where you are, lets you save places, finds help nearby, and shows rain radar, clouds, warnings, disaster alerts and ocean conditions. Some layers work only in the United States; their names say "U.S. only".

                Your privacy

                • No ads. No cookies. It counts anonymous visits (GoatCounter): no cookies, no location data.
                • Your GPS location and your saved places are stored only on this device. There is no Ready Map server, so nothing is sent to us.
                • Two features send a spot to an outside service, and only when you use them: Find nearby (Hospital, Shelter, Police, Fire station, Gas) sends that spot's coordinates to the Overpass API (OpenStreetMap), and if it is slow, to Photon (photon.komoot.io) and Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org), and "Weather warnings" sends your location (or the map center) to the National Weather Service. "Weather news" sends the map center to the National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) to find your local weather office. "Local weather" sends one spot (your GPS spot, or the spot you tapped, or the map center) to the National Weather Service (api.weather.gov) in the U.S., or to Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) outside the U.S.
                • The app only talks to these services:
                  • OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org), for detailed map tiles, names, fonts and symbols, or OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org) if you pick Local names in More tools.
                  • Overpass API, and as backups Photon (komoot) and Nominatim (OpenStreetMap), only when you tap a Find nearby button. Results are kept on this device for 24 hours.
                  • NOAA / National Weather Service, only when you turn on a weather layer.
                  • NOAA ocean services, only when you turn on an Ocean layer: NOAA nowCOAST (nowcoast.noaa.gov) for currents and sea temperature, NOAA Office of Coast Survey charts (gis.charttools.noaa.gov) for depth lines, and the National Weather Service map server (mapservices.weather.noaa.gov) for marine zones. They see the map area you are looking at.
                  • RainViewer, for world radar outside the U.S. (or when you pick World radar), and if NOAA radar does not work.
                  • World layers, only when you turn them on. Each one sees only the map area you are looking at, never your GPS spot: NOAA nowCOAST (nowcoast.noaa.gov) for world clouds (NOAA/NESDIS GMGSI), GDACS (www.gdacs.org) for world disaster alerts (it sends the whole world list; the app picks the nearest ones on your phone), NOAA CoastWatch (coastwatch.noaa.gov) for world currents, and GEBCO (wms.gebco.net) for world sea depth.
                  • Weather news, only when you open it: api.weather.gov (local office, hurricane and tropical news; it gets the map center), www.weather.gov (national headlines) and GDACS (world list). Nothing about you is sent to weather.gov or GDACS.
                  • Local weather, only when you open it (then every 15 minutes while GPS is on): api.weather.gov in the U.S., Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) outside the U.S. It gets that one spot. Outside the U.S. it also gets the GDACS world list. The answer is kept only in memory, never saved on the phone.
                  • Weather stations, only when you turn them on and zoom in: the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (mesonet.agron.iastate.edu) gets the list of stations in view, never your GPS spot.
                  • National radar, only with the radar on and zoomed in over Canada or Germany: Environment Canada (geo.weather.gc.ca) or the DWD (maps.dwd.de). They see the map area you are looking at.
                  • Country lookup (api-bdc.io, BigDataCloud), for the spot's weather, its forecast model and its official weather service link, and for national radar near borders. It gets that one spot, rounded. The answer is kept only in memory.
                  • Google Maps or Apple Maps, only when you tap one of those links.
                • When you use those services, they can see a map area or spot, like any website.
